## Why the positive bicycle review is insufficient

The bicycle source describes checking for embedded sharp debris and visible damage,
states that a visual check cannot guarantee safety, and notes possible professional
assessment. The generated draft adds examples, particular damage types, and advice
to remove debris carefully if safe. These are not literally in the source excerpt.
They might be reasonable background knowledge or cautiously scoped advice; absence
alone does not establish that the advice is false or materially unsafe. However,
the review calls the draft an accurate source summary and describes the specific
advice as reflected in the supplied evidence, without distinguishing source-backed
facts from model-originated elaboration. It does not establish provenance fidelity
for those additions. A positive whole-message verdict is therefore too coarse to
serve as automatic admission evidence.

The language draft accurately describes the supplied synthetic recording notice
in this bounded review. That does not review the later Chinese expression, its
medium-specific wording, real-world truth or usefulness. Draft source support and
expression fidelity remain different questions, neither implied by the other.

Preserve both raw positive results and this limitation. Do not change the authored
labels until ambiguity is examined, accumulate tyre/video blacklists, or silently
replace the default with a previously tested longer prompt. Earlier claim/facet
accounting methods are diagnostic tools, not already qualified superior methods.
Next assess the provenance boundary of generated contextual suggestions separately
from source assertions, including complete expressed content rather than just the
English draft. Any evidence ledger must preserve ambiguity rather than manufacture
truth from exact string matching or agreement between models.
